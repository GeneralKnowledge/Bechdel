# Contract

**Status:** Draft, 15 Nov 2026. Rows marked **Settled** record Reuben's answers of 23 Oct 2026 to the plan's checklist and his earlier choices of direction. Rows marked **Proposed** are recommendations for him to approve or change. Only Reuben changes this file.

| Item | Decision | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Title | *The Grave Signed with Her Hand* | Settled 23 Oct |
| World | The Thousand-Year World: a civilisation about a thousand years after all men disappeared. Women are the whole cast. The absence of men is the premise, never the subject, and is never explained. | Settled |
| Genre | Literary mystery and social history, character-led. Not science fiction: no genetics, labs or futuristic cities as defining features. Technology roughly present-day. | Settled 23 Oct |
| Foundation | The story must grip a reader with no interest in the premise. Women need not be admirable. No sentimental utopia, no thesis novel. | Settled |
| The mystery | Senna Aldous was not killed in 647. She was sentenced to be "made dead to the Keeping" for a forgery she did not commit, lived thirty years in Wexham, and was later misread as a martyr. The tomb holds Jory of the porch. No fire, no faked death, no new name. | Settled 23 Oct |
| Hidden layers | 1. Senna lived (a clever reader may guess). 2. The tomb holds Jory (few will guess). 3. Marit chose to let Senna go and told the children she was dead. | Settled |
| Evidence | Documentary: letters, diaries, registers, court and tribunal records, accounts, gravestones. No document longer than a page on the page. Science alone never settles anything; documents do. | Settled |
| Marit | Knew Senna was alive and in Wexham. Her motive stays open on the page. | Settled 23 Oct |
| Benedicta Teague | Knew of the forgery; she commissioned it. | Settled 23 Oct |
| Senna's voice | Her letters, plus one historical postscript scene no investigator learns (Senna paying the gravedigger and scratching her stroke on Jory's marker). | Settled 23 Oct |
| Interludes | Yes: a few pages each, present tense, for the unwritten (Jory, Bett Langley, the cataloguer, perhaps Hanne Rusk). They never name Senna. Jory's must not reveal where she is buried. | Settled 23 Oct |
| The Order's last decree | Hopeful: copies made in good faith keep. | Settled 23 Oct |
| Tamsin's last act | She refuses to swear the oath again. She and Juno take Jory into their household roll; the Order later copies the entry under its new decree. | Settled 23 Oct |
| Juno and a child | Yes. A space left below Jory's name in their household roll is the last image. | Settled 23 Oct (Reuben delegated; Claude's pick) |
| What stays open | Why Marit let Senna go. The healing at the shrine. Nothing else: the main mystery is answered completely. Ambiguity on motive, never on fact. | Settled |
| Structure | Six movements (I The error, II The pattern, III The wrong answer, IV The missing distinction, V The causal solution, VI The changed world), each opening with an epigraph from the Life: the Great Life until Tamsin finds the 660 Account, Dorcas's Account after. Interludes between movements. Each movement has a pressure from outside the investigation. | Settled (plan) |
| Point of view | Close third person, past tense. **Tamsin** carries the plot (about two thirds of the words). Three secondary POVs for the lives that run without her: **Kate** (the faith from inside; Juno through Kate's eyes), **Perpetua** (the Cause from inside, with Hesketh), **Bryony** (Wexham and the letters). Interludes in present tense. No scene from inside Senna except the postscript. | **Proposed** |
| Not POVs | Juno (seen by Tamsin and Kate), Hesketh (seen by Perpetua), Orla, Delphine, Agnes, Fen, Honor, Win. Fen's columns appear as documents. | **Proposed** |
| Length | 100,000 words is a ceiling, not a target. Storyboard about 94,000: movements of about 14,000, 15,000, 16,000, 17,000, 15,000 and 11,000 (88,000), plus five interludes of about 1,200 each. | **Proposed** |
| Span | From mid-December 1021 to midsummer 1022. Movement I ends at the household Reading on the last night of 1021, which opens the anniversary year. The 375th anniversary of the sentence (9 April 1022) falls in Movement V, at about 70 to 80% of the book. | **Proposed** (see storyboard choice 3) |
| Opening | The orchard, not the faith: Tamsin walking the disputed boundary with two sisters who are not speaking. | Settled (plan guardrail) |
| Ending | Decisions with costs, in the order the plan gives (Kate, Hesketh and Perpetua, the Rooks, Delphine, the oath, taking Jory in). Not a rescue. | Settled (plan) |
| Named beats | The only things protected from criticism: the stroke in the margin of a letter dated 668; Tamsin's wrong answer in Movement III; "made dead to the Keeping, for the rolls" read correctly; the bones identified by documents; Tamsin's refusal of the oath; Jory written into a household roll; the space below her name. Everything else, including every scene, can be cut or rebuilt if a reader shows it fails. | **Proposed** |
| Hindsight | None. Every historical woman acted on what she knew at the time. No document is written to be found. Nobody in 1022 knows more than the documents she has read. | Settled (plan) |
| Author voice | No em dashes. No caption fragments in narration ("She sat. Silent. Final."). No narrator explaining what a scene has shown. No bans or quotas beyond these. British English. | **Proposed** (Reuben's standing rules on *Ides*) |
| Invented names | A new named person needs a row in [canon/names.md](canon/names.md) and a reason. One handle, one person. | **Proposed** |
| Readers | One outside reader per movement on a stamped copy with the questionnaire. At least one who reads mysteries and does not care about the premise. Then one whole-book cold read with nothing protected. | **Proposed** |
