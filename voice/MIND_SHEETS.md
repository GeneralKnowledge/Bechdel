# Mind sheets

**Status:** First version, 15 Nov 2026. Descriptive, not prescriptive. From the plan's cast notes and the method of Reuben's *Ides of March* project, where an outside reader of a finished draft found that "every viewpoint thinks alike": different vocabularies, one way of thinking. These sheets exist so that problem is solved before the first scene, not after the last.

**How to use them.** Read the sheet before writing a scene, then put it away. Nothing here is a rule or a ban. If a sentence could be moved into another woman's scene without anyone noticing, it probably belongs to the author and not to her.

**Three tests for every scene.**

1. **Action.** What actually happens, and what changes because of it?
2. **Point of view.** What would this particular woman notice, misunderstand, conceal or care about?
3. **Explanation.** Is the narration telling the reader something the scene has already shown?

**Two tests for a scene's ending.**

1. Take away the last sentence. Does the scene lose anything it needs? If not, it may be an explanation.
2. End on the physical action instead. What does the reader understand without being told?

Neither is an order to cut endings, and the watchlist adds the other half: not every scene should end on a gesture that carries the theme.

**The aim** is women who are more particular, not plainer prose. Characters are not made less articulate; they are given more to do, more to conceal, and people on whom their words have an effect.

**The risk common to all of them** is the premise. Nobody in 1022 notices that there are no men, any more than a reader notices that there are no dragons. Nobody explains the Keeping to someone who already knows it.

## Present-day viewpoints

### Tamsin

**Notices first:** hands and paper. Ink, the pressure of a nib, a correction, the grain of a page, which way a document has been folded and how many times. In people, what they are holding and whether they are holding it as if it were theirs.

**How a thought moves:** from the mark to the hand to the person. She builds slowly from what is demonstrable and is uncomfortable until she can say "this, therefore this". Her mistakes come from that comfort: she takes demonstrable for true (the plan's phrase), and in Movement III that is exactly how she gets it wrong.

**When she generalises:** about documents, never about people. Her one coinage is professional: she calls a story everyone repeats without checking a "Reading" **(see findings item 10)**.

**What she cannot or will not say:** that she misses the registry, and that the oath still means something to her. She lapsed quietly and does not argue about it.

**What she misunderstands:** that the work is separate from her life. That Juno's question can wait for the end of this job. That Kate will forgive being treated as a source because the truth is the truth.

**In talk:** dry, exact, a beat slow when someone is upset, because she is still reading the situation. Better with Win Corley than with Kate.

**Body:** reading glasses pushed up and forgotten; a callus on her second finger from years of pens; cold hands in archives.

**Sounds like her:** "The stroke turned back on itself at the bottom, the way it did on the chapel rolls, a little hook like a woman catching her skirt on a nail."
**Does not:** "History was only ever the story the winners copied." (A thesis. She would look at the copy.)

### Kate

**Notices first:** bodies and comfort. Who is cold, who has not eaten, who is pretending not to be in pain. On a ward and at a hearth she sees the same things.

**How a thought moves:** sideways, by people. A question about Senna becomes a question about her grandmother, then about who is coming on Sunday. She does not argue doctrine; she lives it.

**When she generalises:** about families, from cases: what happened to Aunt Ros's sister, what a patient said at three in the morning.

**What she cannot or will not say:** that she wants Tamsin back in the faith. That the Cause matters to her because the Aldouses are nobody in Calloway except as Senna's family.

**What she misunderstands:** that being Tamsin's oldest friend means she is not also a source. That the family story is the family.

**In talk:** warm, practical, funny about bodies, blunt when tired. She answers "kept" at the Reading without performing it.

**Body:** ward shoes; a nurse's watch pinned upside down; she stands when others sit.

**The risk:** she is the plan's chosen voice for "a woman doing three things at once" about Marit. Give her that as her own family's argument, said to someone with a stake in it, not as the book's verdict (watchlist question 8).

### Perpetua

**Notices first:** letterforms. Ductus, ligatures, the angle of a pen, a scribe's habit of abbreviation. Then institutions: who signed, who countersigned, whose interest the wording serves.

**How a thought moves:** in objections. She doubts for a living and enjoys it, and her loyalty to the Order shows only in how hard she checks. She holds two thoughts and does not hurry either.

**When she generalises:** about scribes and about Hesketh, both with affection.

**What she cannot or will not say:** that she would like the Cause to succeed for Hesketh's sake.

**What she misunderstands:** that her office protects her. That doubt is a service the Order will thank her for.

**In talk:** precise, teasing with Hesketh, formal with everyone else. Thirty years of the same argument with one friend have given them a shorthand.

**Body:** she reads with a glass, and her hands are steadier than her knees.

### Bryony

**Notices first:** weather, trees, the state of a gate. Then people's hands on her things.

**How a thought moves:** slowly, in tasks. She thinks while doing something and decides in the middle of it.

**When she generalises:** rarely, and in the meeting house's words, which she knows by heart.

**What she cannot or will not say:** that the orchard quarrel is about their mother's funeral. That the meeting-house letters are "Grandmother Senna's", which she considers nobody's business.

**What she misunderstands:** that giving Tamsin the letters keeps them inside the family.

**In talk:** short, plain, Wexham. Not quick. Funny without trying when she answers exactly what was asked.

**Body:** pruning cuts on her hands; mud on her boots in the kitchen; she reads aloud better than she talks.

## Interludes

Present tense, a few pages each, in the voices the record left out: Jory on the porch, Bett on the day she took the steward's money, the cataloguer at her desk, perhaps Hanne Rusk. They never name Senna. Each reads on first meeting as atmosphere and on second reading as evidence. Jory's must not reveal where she is buried.

The risk is beauty used as a signal. If the interludes are visibly the best-written pages, readers will search them for clues. Write them as plainly as the women would think.
