# Drafting watchlist

Questions for the self-audit at the end of each movement, after the three tests in [MIND_SHEETS.md](MIND_SHEETS.md). They are not bans or quotas, and none of them is a reason to make the prose less polished, less articulate or less literary.

Questions 1 to 5 are the watchlist Reuben's *Ides of March* project wrote after an outside editorial review of its Book 1, Chapters 1 to 6 (11 Nov 2026): habits of his house style to manage, not eliminate. Questions 6 to 8 come from that project's criticisms ledger, and 9 and 10 from this book's plan.

## Ten questions

1. **Spacing of good lines.** Read a scene's dialogue aloud. If every speaker lands a quotable line, which one is this woman's and which are the book's? Keep the best one. Let the others misunderstand, repeat themselves, trail off or say the ordinary thing, where that is what she would do. Do not manufacture awkwardness.
2. **Reversals.** Look for the turn "not this, but that", the thing that turns out to mean its opposite. Once in a scene is thought. Twice is a shape. Change how the second thought moves (a question, an interruption, an action) and keep the insight. This book's premise is a record that means the opposite of what it seems, so the risk is high.
3. **Scene endings.** If every scene ends on a gesture that carries the theme, readers start waiting for it. Across a movement, let some scenes stop when the business is done: someone leaves, the door shuts, the next thing on the list.
4. **Minor characters.** A walk-on gets eloquence only when she would have it: education, rank, drink, nerve, a reason to show off. A bus driver, a porter, a choir alto talks like one. Honor Bale, who has read the Great Life aloud for forty years, is allowed to sound like it.
5. **Visible design.** Echoes and parallels are the book's structure and stay (the two graves, the two refusals, the two Lives). Each scene must also work as an encounter between people who want something from each other, with no knowledge of the scenes around it. If a scene is there only to complete a parallel, give it its own trouble or cut it.
6. **The motif that saturates.** In *Ides*, debt became how every viewpoint summed up its position. Here the word is *kept*: keeping, kept, keeper, keepsake. Let its meaning keep changing (the ritual, the oath, a household, a secret, a copy) and do not let it be how every woman sums up her position.
7. **The investigation that runs too smoothly.** Does every document arrive when needed and get read correctly at once? Somebody should misread, be refused access, find the wrong box, or be right for the wrong reason. See the misreadings table in [mystery/theories.md](../mystery/theories.md).
8. **A minor character who interprets the lead.** Is a non-POV woman handed the sentence that explains Tamsin's moral position, or the book's verdict on Marit? Give her the line only if it is her own family's or her own trade's, said to someone, with a consequence.
9. **The present against the past.** Is this movement's present-day thread as gripping as its documents? The comparable novels (*People of the Book*, *Alias Grace*) lose readers in the present. Every present-day scene needs a want and a cost that is not about the past.
10. **Documents.** Is every document under a page, worth reading for its own sake, and written by someone who did not know it would be found?

## Standing rules (not questions)

- No em dashes.
- No caption fragments in narration: a full sentence followed by one-word or verbless fragments that label it ("She closed the book. Soft. Final."). Fold it into the sentence or give it a subject and verb. Speech may clip as people do.
- No narrator explaining what the scene has shown.
- No sentence explains the absence of men or the Keeping to a reader who does not share it.

## How to use it

At the movement's self-audit, read each scene once for these ten, after the three tests. Note what you change in the changelog line for the movement. If a question turns up nothing, move on. Criticisms from outside readers that touch the same habits go in [reader_feedback/OPEN_CRITICISMS.md](../reader_feedback/OPEN_CRITICISMS.md) and are counted.
