# Everyday life

**Status:** First version, 15 Nov 2026. Free to extend; anything here may be contradicted by canon, and canon wins.

## The year

Ordinary months and weekdays. The era is counted from the founding of the first rolls; nobody thinks about why, any more than we think about why it is 2026.

| When | What | Who cares |
|---|---|---|
| 31 December | **The Reading.** Every household reads its roll at the hearth, oldest name first; the living answer the dead with "kept". Then food. Offices close at noon; buses stop at eight. | Everyone, including people who never go to chapel, the way people who never pray still keep Christmas |
| 1 January | A dead day. Leftovers, walks, rows about whose turn it is to host next year. | |
| Late January (the 27th) | **Hild's Day.** The feast of Saint Hild of the Door. Schools put on the children's play: the raiders, the door, Hild with her back to it. Every child in Calloway has been a raider at least once. Bread rolls baked in the shape of a door bolt. | Parents, teachers, bakers |
| Naming Days | Whenever a child is written into a household roll; the hymn is sung. Not a fixed date. | |
| 9 April | **Senna's Day.** The anniversary of the sentence, kept in Calloway as the day of the saint's death since about 800. A civic holiday in the city only, not in Wexham. Honor Bale reads the death scene from the Great Life in the Registry Chapel. In 1022, the 375th: a concert, the translation of the bones to a new shrine, a souvenir tea towel. | Calloway; the Order; the *Courier*; souvenir sellers |
| April | Pear blossom in Wexham. Frost nights: growers sit up with burners in the rows. | Bryony, every grower in the valley |
| Midsummer (24 June) | **Midsummer chapter.** The Order's yearly general meeting, where decrees are voted. Also the longest evening, picnics, school's-out feeling a month early. | The Order; nobody else, except as a day off |
| Early October | **Return Day** in Wexham: a fair, cider, a procession of the smallholders' rolls round the market square. The reason (the 649 judgment that gave the eastern land back) is in every local schoolbook and nobody remembers it. | Wexham |

## Work and households

Households are what everything runs on. A household keeps a roll; its members share its name; property passes by the roll. A **hearth bond** joins the women who keep a household together, and is as ordinary and as serious as marriage is to us. Households also take in sisters, aunts and old friends who would otherwise live alone, and nobody thinks that odd. Children are written into the household roll on their Naming Day.

Jobs are jobs. The Registry is a civic office, paid by the city, but its registrars are ordained by the Order and swear the keepers' oath, a leftover from the centuries when the two were one. Freelance title researchers like Tamsin need no ordination; she keeps hers the way some people keep a lapsed membership card.

## Health

The **Calloway Infirmary** is the general hospital, on the same site as the medieval infirmary whose books survive in the city archive. Wards are short of night staff, as everywhere. Kate works on a medical ward: twelve-hour shifts, three on, four off, and an extra night whenever someone rings in sick. Pharmacies are where people go first; Orla runs one on Wexham high street and knows everyone's prescriptions, which she considers a burden and a power.

## Schools

Children go to school from five to eighteen. Music is the first thing cut. Juno teaches music at **Holm Lane School**, a large secondary on the east side of Calloway, and runs its choir as well as the anniversary choir, which is paid separately and badly.

## News

The ***Calloway Courier***: a daily, thinner every year, more read online than in print, with a weekend magazine. Its comment section is the city's real parliament. Local radio does traffic, weather and phone-ins on parking. Fen Archer's anniversary column runs on Saturdays.

## Food

The Reading has its own: a raised pie, pickled Wexham pears, and **keeping loaf**, a dense spiced bread that keeps for weeks and is eaten until March out of duty. In January everyone is sick of it. Hild's Day has the bolt-shaped rolls. The Infirmary canteen does a breakfast that the night staff defend against all criticism.

## Getting about

Buses, cars, bicycles, trains to the capital two hours away. Tamsin does not drive; Juno has the car and needs it for school. Every trip Tamsin makes to Wexham is a bus or a favour, and she is bad at asking for favours.

## Phones and paper

Everyone has a phone and takes photographs of everything. Archives forbid it, then allow it for a fee, then forget to check. Rolls are still paper by law: a roll kept only on a computer does not count at the Registry, and the argument about whether a household roll on a laptop "keeps" is one of the faith's livelier quarrels ([calloway.md](calloway.md)).
