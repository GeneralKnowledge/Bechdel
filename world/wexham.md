# Wexham

**Status:** First version, 15 Nov 2026. Canon places are in [canon/places.md](../canon/places.md).

A market town of about nine thousand, forty minutes east of Calloway on the 22, in a valley of small orchards and smaller farms. One high street, a market square with a stone cross, a closed bank that is now a pizza place, the Plain Keeping meeting house, a primary school on the site of the old schoolhouse, and a secondary school everyone takes a bus to. People who live there say it is quiet. People who visit say it is dead.

## Work

Pears, mainly, and some apples; cider; a packing house; a garden centre; the pharmacy; the school; commuting to Calloway. Small growers have been going out of business for thirty years, and orchards with lane frontage are being sold for houses. Everyone has an opinion on this and most of them are about somebody else's orchard.

The pear year: pruning in January and February in the cold; grafting in March; blossom in April and the frost nights, when growers sit up with burners in the rows and phone each other at three in the morning to compare thermometers; thinning in June; picking from August. Bryony's year runs on this, and she would be doing it whether or not anyone had ever heard of Senna.

## The Plain Keeping as a neighbour

In Wexham the Plain Keeping is not a cause or a grievance. It is the meeting house on Tanner's Row, where about two hundred people go on a Sunday, a plain room with no rolls on the walls and good heating, and fewer to the Saturday evening reading, which is where the readers practise on each other. Plain Keepers keep household rolls like everyone else but do not hold that the paper does the keeping; a name is kept while someone remembers it. They ask no papers of anyone. They read aloud from a small set of old texts, the best loved of which is *The Schoolmistress's Letters*, and members take turns to read. Bryony is one of the readers, and better at it than anyone expects.

Their quarrels are local and their own: whether to replace the chairs with pews (no); whether to let the Calloway Order's people use the room for a joint service in the anniversary year (a vote, narrowly yes, with a long silence afterwards); whether the reading rota is fair (never).

Most of Wexham is not Plain Keeping at all. There is an ordinary Order chapel at the top of the high street, with its own small congregation, and the two sit side by side at the Return Day fair.

## Wexham and the eastern land

Every Wexham child learns that in 649 a Calloway court gave the eastern smallholdings back to the people who worked them, and that this is why there is a fair in October. Nobody connects it with anything else. The Rook orchard is one of those smallholdings, and the family's own version is that "the Rooks have had the pear row since the Return", which is true and which nobody has tested.

## The orchard

Four acres on Pell Lane, on the eastern edge of the town, in two titles: the **upper parcel**, along the lane, left to Orla, and the **lower parcel**, running down to the brook, left to Bryony. Their mother left it that way to be fair and did not live to see it. Between the two parcels runs the **pear row**: thirty old trees, some over a century, the best fruit on the land.

On the Registry roll the line between the parcels runs down the middle of the pear row, giving each sister half the trees. Neither believes it. Orla's builder needs the whole row for a turning circle. Bryony needs it because the row is the orchard. The family has always said the row belongs to the lower parcel, along a line that follows the row's eastern edge. That is the line in the letter of 668 ([canon/places.md](../canon/places.md)).

## Small things

- The 22 stops at the market cross, and its last bus back to Calloway goes at seven, which is when every visitor's conversation ends.
- The pub is the Wheatsheaf; the café is Pell's, which has nothing to do with Pell Lane and is tired of being asked.
- Wexham people call Calloway "town" and never go there if they can help it.
- The Plain Keeping burial ground is behind the meeting house, small, walled, full of plain stones with first names and a trade. Families tend their own.
