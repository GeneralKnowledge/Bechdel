# What this project takes from *The Ides of March*

Reuben's earlier novel project ([GeneralKnowledge/Ides](https://github.com/GeneralKnowledge/Ides)) wrote Book 1 first and then built Books 2 and 0 with a leaner method shaped by what went wrong on Book 1. This file records which of those lessons are used here, where they live in this repository, and which were left behind on purpose. Read 15 Nov 2026 at Ides commit `2c1bb7d`.

## What Book 1 taught

Book 1 was drafted chapter by chapter from beat sheets and scene cards, then improved through more than a hundred logged passes (`archive/pass_logs/` in Ides). Three things came out of that:

1. **The voice problem was found late.** An outside reader of the finished draft found that "the prose explains itself and every viewpoint thinks alike". Fixing it meant suspending a large body of rules and rewriting scenes from new mind sheets. Books 2 and 0 settled each viewpoint's way of thinking *before* any prose, and tested it blind.
2. **Rules and passes accumulated.** Human-voice rules, pass caps, protected chapters, detector-led scrubs, pass logs for every change. Some of them protected the very habits readers later criticised. Books 2 and 0 kept a short list of standing rules, one changelog line per change and no pass logs.
3. **Outside readers were the only reliable signal, but they had to be counted.** Seven reads that turned out to be one reviewer were briefly treated as seven. The fix was a ledger that counts *sources*, and acts on a criticism once three separate reads raise it.

## Left behind on purpose

- **Detector-led prose passes** and anything aimed at scoring as "less AI". Ides's own rules ended up saying: prefer a cold read that feels human over a scrub that scores better.
- **Pass caps, protected chapters and long rule files.** They were the scaffolding of a draft-first process and some of them protected the habits readers criticised.
- **Beat sheets and scene cards separate from the storyboard.** One TSV replaces them.
- **Pass logs and editorial reports** as a record. The changelog and the criticisms ledger are enough.
- **Fixed historical anchors** (Ides's "history first, invent only where the record is silent"). This world is invented. The nearest equivalent is the chronology: once a document's date, wording or location is in [canon/chronology.md](../canon/chronology.md), it is fixed like history.
- **Ebook, audiobook and print-imposition tooling.** Useful at the end, not now.
