# Dorcas Pryor, An Account of Senna Aldous (660)

**Source:** One surviving copy, Order library; seldom requested (III-01). Written by Dorcas Pryor, keeper since 655, about her friend, while the Order was watching. Plain, cautious, oddly evasive. It never says Senna died. Dorcas did not write it to be decoded: she wrote what she could defend before the chapter and nothing she would have to lie about.

**Clues:** C07. Settled wording, 15 Nov 2026.

---

## The passage Tamsin and Perpetua read (III-01)

Senna Aldous was entered in the Registry of Calloway in the year six hundred and thirty-five, being nineteen years of age, and served there under Keeper Carrow twelve years. She wrote a small hand and a clear one, and kept the eastern rolls.

In the winter of six hundred and forty-six she made fair copies of the eastern rolls, at night and at her own charge, the old rolls being in such a state as she judged them unfit to be kept. In the spring following a question was raised about those copies before the tribunal of the Order, and judgment was given in the ninth day of April. Senna Aldous was made dead for the rolls, and the house of the Keeping was the poorer.

Her copies were afterwards brought before the city court in the matter of the eastern lands, in six hundred and forty-nine, and were found good, and are kept by the grace of the Keeper as the rolls of those lands. I set down that they were found good. I set down also that I knew her hand, and know it.

She was my friend. I have nothing to add to what is written.

## Note

The Account is the source of the Great Life's "made dead … and the house of the Keeping was the poorer". Oriel Vance, writing about 800, read "made dead for the rolls" as a death, as anyone would who no longer knew the sentence, and wrote Hild's death around it.
