# The death of Saint Hild of the Door

**Source:** The old legend of Saint Hild, much older than the Great Life, known in Calloway as the Hild's Day reading and from the children's play (II-07). The text here is the one the Order library holds, which Perpetua sets beside the Great Life in III-02. Every child in Calloway knows the last line.

**Clues:** C08 (the Great Life's death scene borrows this one almost line for line; see [0800_great_life_death.md](0800_great_life_death.md)). Settled wording, 15 Nov 2026.

---

And when the raiders came up from the river in the night, Hild was alone in the roll-house with the lamp. She heard them at the gate, and she did not run. She set her back against the door and her hand upon the bolt, and she called to the keepers who slept in the loft to take the rolls out by the garden way.

They struck the door with axes, and the door held, for Hild held it. They struck it until the lamp went out, and still she called the names of the households to those who carried them, that none be left behind.

When the last roll was out the door gave, and they found her on the floor of the empty house. She said, *There is nothing here of yours.* And she said, *Write me where the fire is kept.* And she died.

In the morning the keepers counted the rolls, and not one name was lost. Read her, for she was read.
