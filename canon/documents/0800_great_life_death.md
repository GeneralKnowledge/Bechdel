# The Great Life: the death of Senna

**Source:** Oriel Vance's Life of Senna Aldous, written about 800 during the schism with the Plain Keepers. Read aloud every Senna's Day in the Registry Chapel; Honor Bale has read it for forty years (I-06, III-03, V-02). Printed in the chapel shop's edition. Ornate, certain and moving; written to hold a faith together, by a woman who believed it.

**Clues:** C08 (its death is Hild's: compare [older_hild_of_the_door.md](older_hild_of_the_door.md)), C09 (Honor's two added lines, below). Settled wording, 15 Nov 2026.

---

Now in the spring of the year six hundred and forty-seven there came against the Registry of Calloway certain of a great house, who had corrupted the rolls of the eastern lands and feared the truth of them. And they came up from the river in the night, when Senna Aldous was alone among the rolls with the lamp.

She heard them at the gate, and she did not run. She set her back against the door and her hand upon the bolt, and she called to her sisters of the Registry to carry the true rolls out by the chapel way.

They struck the door with staves, and the door held, for Senna held it. They struck it until the lamp went out, and still she named the rolls to those who carried them, the eastern lands and the western, that none be left behind.

When the last roll was out the door gave, and they found her upon the floor of the empty room. She said, *There is nothing here of yours.* And she said, *Write me where the fire is kept.* And she died for the rolls, being made dead in the thirty-first year of her age, and the house of the Keeping was the poorer.

And the rolls she saved are with us unto this day, and her hand is upon them. Read her, for she is read.

---

## Honor Bale's two lines

Honor adds these, after "Write me where the fire is kept", and has for so long that she no longer knows they are hers. They are not in the printed text. Juno has heard them every week since September and did not know they were new (III-03).

> *And her hand was on the bolt, and her hand was steady.*
> *She asked no one's name, for she knew them all.*

## Epigraphs from the Great Life

| Movement | Passage |
|---|---|
| I | "And the rolls she saved are with us unto this day, and her hand is upon them." |
| II | "She set her back against the door and her hand upon the bolt." |
| III | "And she died for the rolls, being made dead in the thirty-first year of her age, and the house of the Keeping was the poorer." |

Oriel Vance borrowed the last words of that sentence, unknowing, from Dorcas's Account (see [0660_dorcas_account.md](0660_dorcas_account.md)), where "made dead" still means what the tribunal meant. The reader meets the phrase three times before IV: in the order of service as "for the rolls" (II-03), here (III epigraph), and in the Account (III-01).
