# Senna's letter about the Hollis child, 659

**Source:** Rook family box; also among *The Schoolmistress's Letters* read at the Wexham meeting house, where it is the best known. Bryony reads it aloud in I-04. Senna writes to Lise Rook, who is away for a fortnight nursing her sister. One word is struck out and corrected in the margin with the hooked stroke.

**Clues:** C04 (the meeting-house letters). Settled wording, 15 Nov 2026.

---

Lise,

The Hollis child has made her capitals backwards for a month and I have decided to let her keep them. She makes them backwards perfectly. Every R faces the door. I asked her why, and she said so that they can see who is coming, which is a better reason than most of the clerks I have known had for anything.

Her mother came up to the schoolhouse on Thursday to ask whether a name written backwards in the roll would be kept. I said I did not know of any rule. She said there must be one. I said that if the Keeper cannot read a child's R facing the wrong way then the Keeper has less sense than the child's mother, and she went away comforted, or offended; with her it is the same face.

I am not sure I believe the Keeper reads. I am sure that you and I can, and the child's mother, and the child. Perhaps that is enough to be going on with. You will tell me it is the meeting house talking. It is the stove talking, and the stove says you were right about the flue.

The plums are over. Ada has eaten most of them and says the wasps did it.

Come home when your sister lets you, and not before. We are managing, which is to say the bread is bad.

Senna
