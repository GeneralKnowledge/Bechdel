# Senna's letter about the lower field, 668

**Source:** Rook family box. The letter Bryony puts on the kitchen table in I-05 to settle the boundary, and the first in which Tamsin sees the stroke. Senna writes to Lise Rook, who is in Calloway on household business, about letting the lower field. The hooked stroke corrects a figure in the margin.

**Clues:** C01 (the stroke, the signature, the date), C02 (the boundary follows the forged line along the eastern edge of the pear row; see [canon/places.md](../places.md)). Settled wording, 15 Nov 2026.

---

The 9th of May, 668

Lise,

The Tench girl came about the lower field and I have let it to her for the summer on your terms, not hers. Grazing only, from the brook to the pear row and the pear row with it, to its eastern edge, as your mother worked it. She is not to put a beast under the trees until the fruit is set, and not to cut so much as a twig. I made her say it back. She said it back as if it were her Naming Day.

Rent at the quarter, eleven shillings. She offered nine and a promise. I took the eleven.

Ada has a cold and is enjoying it. The school has the same cold and is enjoying it less. I have stopped the copybooks for a week and we are doing sums out loud, which is a mercy for the paper and nobody else.

Do not let them keep you in town past Thursday. Town will always have one more paper for you to sign.

Senna
