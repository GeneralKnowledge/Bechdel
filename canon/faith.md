# The Keeping

**Status:** First version, 15 Nov 2026, from the plan doc. The rules the plot depends on. Texture that is not connected to the mystery (food at the Reading, the chapel-music quarrel, the Order's property scandals, the two rival parish choirs) is free to invent, and should be: most of the faith has nothing to do with the mystery.

## The one idea

To be written is to be kept. A person endures in the Keeper's care for as long as her name stands in a roll and is read aloud.

## Practices the plot uses

| Practice | Rule | Where the plot uses it |
|---|---|---|
| **Rolls** | Every household keeps a roll. The Order keeps the public rolls. Land titles are entered in the registry rolls. | Everywhere |
| **The Reading** | Once a year each household reads its roll by the hearth, oldest name first. The living answer each name of the dead with "kept". Held on the last night of the year. | I-04 (Kate's family); Kate's faith at its most persuasive; Tamsin's voice catches |
| **The hymn** | Sung at every Naming Day: *Write her where the fire is kept, / Read her when the house is still; / Though the hand that wrote her fail, / Another hand will, another will.* The last line is about copying. | I-02, I-08; turns in VI |
| **The keepers' oath** | At ordination a registrar swears "I keep it twice, by Senna's rolls". Tamsin swore it at 22. | VI: Tamsin refuses to renew it |
| **Taking in** | A household may write into its own roll a dead person who was never kept (a foundling, a stranger who died under its roof). No keeper, no oath: only the household's hearth. Rare and solemn. | Planted in I-04; the ending |
| **Hearth bond** | The bond between partners who keep a household together. In the Order it is dissolved only by death or by the sentence. Plain Keepers may release it by consent. | Marit and Senna; Tamsin and Juno |
| **Being made dead** | A tribunal sentence. The name moves to the section of the dead in every roll; the hearth bond is dissolved, as death dissolves it; the household reads her name with the dead; she may not enter a Keeping chapel. She is alive, unkept and free to go. Most go to the dissenting towns. By 1022 rare and slightly shameful to mention, like excommunication; few know the old wording. | The whole mystery |
| **Original by grace** | Since 649 the Order may declare a copy an original. Only the Order. | 649 decree; Agnes's fear in 1022; the final decree |
| **The Cause** | The Order's process for elevating a saint: an Advocate (Hesketh) argues for it; an Examiner (Perpetua) is paid to doubt; the Order votes. | I-07 onwards |

## The Plain Keeping

The Keeper keeps people in living memory; paper is a convenience. Hearth bonds can be released by mutual consent. They ask no papers. In 649 and again about 800, the Order settled doctrine in ways that also settled property, and the Plain Keepers lost both times and were pushed into provincial respectability. Wexham's meeting house has read Senna's letters for three hundred years as *The Schoolmistress's Letters*, by "a teacher of our town".

## Who gets silenced

Status, not gender. The unwritten (Jory). The unordained (Bett). The dissenters. The poor, whose graves were in the field the Order dug up.

## Writing the faith

From inside, with real beauty, so believers and sceptics both find it persuasive. Intelligent believers, no villains. Tamsin lives inside her world and finds it ordinary: no sentence explains the Keeping to a reader who does not share it.
